## Overview

Develop the mathematical tools needed to study machine learning through probability, optimization, information theory and stochastic analysis. Emphasis is placed on assumptions, proof techniques and the limits of asymptotic results.

**Duration**: 14 weeks · Graduate level  
**Prerequisites**: Measure-theoretic probability, linear algebra, real analysis and introductory optimization.

## Weekly schedule

| Weeks | Research topics |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Measure-theoretic probability, conditional expectation, concentration inequalities, stochastic processes |
| 3–4 | High-dimensional probability, random matrix theory, spectral analysis, introductory free probability |
| 5–6 | Convex analysis, nonconvex optimization, variational analysis, stochastic optimization, implicit regularization |
| 7–8 | Information theory, PAC-Bayes, algorithmic stability, generalization bounds, information bottleneck |
| 9–10 | Bayesian inference, variational inference, statistical physics, free energy, Gibbs distributions |
| 11–12 | Optimal transport, Wasserstein geometry, continuity equations, gradient flows |
| 13–14 | Stochastic differential equations, Fokker–Planck equations, stochastic control, diffusion processes |

## Learning objectives

- Understand the central mathematical objects, algorithms and research methods.
- Identify assumptions, applicability limits and failure conditions.
- Connect literature review, theoretical analysis and reproducible experiments.

## Research assignment

Prove a central result, construct a counterexample that tests its assumptions, and compare theoretical predictions with finite-scale experiments.

## Deliverables

Submit a research report with the necessary code or proofs. State the problem, related work, method, evidence and limitations.
